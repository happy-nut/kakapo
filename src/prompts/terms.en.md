Look back over the conversation we have just had, and record any concept I have taken into **my own words**.

Call `kakapo_words` first. What it returns is the language I actually use about this repository, and every later explanation of it is written in those words.

If there is anything to keep, put it in with `kakapo_keep_word`, one call per word — do not touch any file. The tool knows where the vocabulary lives, and turns away a word already in it or an identifier passed off as one.

What you keep is the whole job.

- **Only words I wrote.** Never a name you coined, however much better it is. If I never said it, it is your concept, not mine.
- **Only what I took in.** I took it in if I stopped asking the same thing, or went on to use the word myself. A word I am still asking about does not go in — that would file a misunderstanding as knowledge.
- **Concepts, not names.** A file, function or variable name is not a word; it is what a word turns out to BE in the code, which is the `code` half of the line.
- **A word that only means something inside another** gets `"parent":"the outer concept"` — "serialization" as we discussed it inside "external API", say.
- **One line of meaning, in my words.** Do not move your whole explanation in. Where that line mentions another word from the file, the map draws a link — so prefer words already in there when you write it.

**If there is nothing to keep, do nothing.** Most conversations are like that. A vocabulary full of words I never chose is worse than an empty one, because the next explanation gets written in them.

When you are done, tell me in one line what you kept.
